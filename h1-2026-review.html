What worked,
what didn't

Six months, two stores, three channels. Judged on revenue alone the half reads as one good sale and a lot of noise. Judged properly, the messages explaining our products are doing better than the sales numbers suggest, and we keep stopping short of asking for the sale.
Finding 01
2× the reads

Messages explaining the product get read twice as much as messages with an offer — 36.6% against 18.5%. They are the best-read thing we send. Sales figures can't show that, so they look like failures on a revenue report.

Finding 02
2%

We explain the product to everyone, then ask almost nobody to buy. In June India told 77,515 people about the product and offered 1,542 of them a deal. The US did the same with Champi hair oil. Both times the small offer was the best send of the month.

Finding 03
26 of 28

Reach is not the constraint. Offer sends were profitable 26 times out of 28 — including to 100K and 196K people. Big sends are not the problem we thought they were.

The half was one sale and four quiet months. Outside April and May the US ran at about $6.6K of CRM revenue a month. That baseline is the number to move, not the peak.
What worked
The Summer Sale had a real structure $31.5K

Waitlist, early access, then a dated close. The closing phase returned 22% of the sale on its own. This is the only campaign shape this half that produced at scale, and it is the template for DKBS'26.

Subscriptions compounded 104 → 466

The one part of the account that grew without a promotion behind it. Recurring orders up 4x, and June reversed May's first negative month.

Flows track traffic reliably $48 / 1K sessions

Flow revenue held between $35 and $58 per thousand sessions every single month. The plumbing is sound and predictable — which means added depth should convert straight through.

What didn't
Flows are thin, not broken $0.22

Per new customer, across 9,067 of them, from every post-purchase flow combined. Welcome earns $373 a month against roughly 4,800 opt-ins. The gap is coverage and depth, not deliverability or setup.

Champi never got a real offer 235K → 3,774

Five emails about Champi hair oil went to 235,000 inboxes in June. The only Champi deal went to 3,774 people, and it got the best clicks of the month and 27% of June's revenue. We built the interest and never asked for the sale.

February bought a bad cohort $32 AOV

2,083 new orders at half the usual basket, and still the worst LTV on record at $28 by month 4 against $54 for January. Worth understanding before repeating whatever drove it.

The Klaviyo pause was not a CRM blackout. Email went dark Feb 17 to May 31, but WhatsApp ran a full Summer Sale through it and April became the strongest CRM month of the half. The tracker only records WhatsApp from June, so H1 has been understated by about ₹23L.
What worked
WhatsApp is a primary channel now ₹17.4L net

Net of ₹5.5L in send cost, across 49 broadcasts. Profitable in every month except June. It should be budgeted and planned as a lead channel, not a supplement.

Big sends paid, when they carried an offer ₹5.1L net

The single biggest contributor was Summer Sale early access to 100,000 existing customers. Every size band was net positive. The instinct to cut reach is the wrong lesson from this half.

Retries are doing real work 5.30x

The second-attempt pass returned ₹5.0L net on its own, at a better multiple than the original send. Worth treating as a deliberate part of the plan rather than a fallback.

What didn't
The June run never got an offer 77,515 → 1,542

Ten messages about MSS went to 77,515 people and a third read them. Then the only deal of the month went to 1,542 people and made 11x what it cost to send. We had everyone's attention and asked two percent of them to buy.

Three April sends can't be judged at all 193K reach

Three unnamed broadcasts went to 193,000 people two days before Summer Sale carrying no link, so nothing can attribute to them by design. If that was deliberate pre-sale warming it needs a name and a measure. Right now ₹70K of spend is unreadable.

Email's three dark months are unmeasurable Feb 17 – May 31

No Brevo attribution was captured, so we cannot say whether email underperformed or simply went unrecorded. We should not read those months either way.

The pattern in both stores

We tell everyone about the product. Almost nobody gets a reason to buy.

June, both stores, same shape. A run of messages explaining the product went to the whole list, and plenty of people read them. Then the one message with an actual offer in it went to a tiny slice. Both times that small offer was the best send of the month. Explaining the product is working. We just stop short of asking for the sale.

What we change in H2
01

End every run with an offer

If we spend a month explaining a product, the deal at the end goes to everyone who heard it. Not two percent of them.

02

Judge those messages on reads

They exist to build interest, so reads and unsubscribes tell us if they worked. Sales figures never will.

03

Fix WhatsApp delivery

36% first-attempt delivery is the cheapest available gain. Template health and window timing before new creative.

04

Deepen US post-purchase

$0.22 per new customer across 9,067 of them. Flows already scale with traffic — there just aren't enough of them.

05

Run DKBS'26 on the sale shape

Waitlist, early access, dated close — the one structure that produced at scale, on email and WhatsApp together.
